How does the management fee work?
Co-Host Solutions charges 20% based on net accommodation revenue — meaning after platform commissions are deducted. So if Agoda takes their 17% cut from a ₱2,000 booking first (leaving ₱1,660), the management fee is 20% of that net amount. This is why the fee varies month to month depending on the platform mix. December had the highest fee (₱3,183) because revenue was highest.

Booking Channels
Who's sending
guests your way?
Your unit is listed on four platforms. Here's how each one works, what it costs, and why the mix matters for your bottom line.
Airbnb
Commission: 4% host fee
The lowest-commission platform you're on. Airbnb tends to attract guests willing to pay premium rates — your highest per-night bookings typically come through here, especially on peak and holiday dates.
Booking.com
Commission: 16.5% per booking
Strong volume driver — good for mid-to-higher rate nights. That ₱4,389 booking on Feb 5 came through here. The 16.5% cut is significant though — a ₱2,000 night means ₱330 goes to Booking.com before anything else.
Agoda
Commission: 17% per booking
Highest booking volume overall, particularly strong with domestic Filipino travelers and Southeast Asian tourists. Your ₱8,298 booking on Feb 20 was likely a multi-night Agoda stay. At 17%, it's the most expensive platform but drives consistent occupancy.
Direct Booking
Commission: 0% — you keep it all
Present every single month and growing. Direct rates have ranged from ₱1,900 to ₱4,400 per night. Every direct booking saves you 16.5–17% compared to OTA bookings — that's a significant difference per stay.
Why does this matter to you?
When a guest books through Agoda at ₱2,000/night, you effectively receive ₱1,660 after their 17% cut. The same guest booking directly means you receive the full ₱2,000. Growing direct bookings is the single biggest lever available to increase your monthly net income without needing more guests — and it's already happening naturally as guests return or refer friends.
